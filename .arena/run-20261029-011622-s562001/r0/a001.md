# What Savi's 541 calls teach the Kesrox WhatsApp off-hours agent

## 1. Read this first (the 60-second version)

1. **Most of the data is not "new lead" talk.** Of 541 calls, about two thirds are existing-customer chasing (payment, dispatch, bilty/LR, stock), internal calls with "Tarun Sir", courier/transport coordination, personal calls, wrong numbers or empty recordings. Only about 60 calls are Savi cold-calling Google/Facebook/IndiaMART leads, and only a handful (#293, #295, #78, #79, #528, #531) are real two-way PCD/third-party conversations. Section 5 gives the counts and how they were made.
2. **Savi always calls out. Nobody here wrote first, at night or in text.** The data shows what Kesrox's contacts care about. It does not show how a stranger opens a WhatsApp chat at 9 pm. Use the findings for content (answers, vocabulary, what to capture), not for conversation flow or timing.
3. **Three things dominate every conversation:** (a) "send me the list/catalog/rate list on WhatsApp", (b) "is it in stock / when will it come", (c) money: rate pushback, advance vs credit, which account/QR to pay, pending payment. An off-hours agent that does these three well covers most of what real contacts do.
4. **The agent must never promise stock, dispatch dates, rates or credit.** In the calls, stock is chronically short, rates keep rising, and delivery promises ("next week") are the biggest customer complaint (#345, #35, #121).
5. **Several facts in the current 5 Q&As are not backed by any real call** (30/70 payment split, "free" marketing kit, free 3D carton design, token-to-book-monopoly, Rs 25,000 opening order). They either contradict what Savi said or appear only in a few scripted-looking calls. Confirm them with the business before the agent states them (section 8).
6. **Hand over to a human with a structured note, not a promise of a call at 10:00.** Section 7 lists what to capture.

## 2. How clients express their needs

All quotes are exact Hinglish from the transcripts. Speaker labels in the transcripts are often swapped ("Employee"/"Customer" are wrong in #78 and #451, for example), so I give call numbers and judge the speaker from context.

### 2.1 The opening
- **The contact usually does not start with a need.** On Savi's cold calls the pitch is always the same: "Sir, koi requirement hai aapko PCD ya third-party manufacturing ke liye?" (#129, #148, #149, #154) or "Sir aapko koi medicine ki requirement hai wholesale ya retail ke liye?" (#118, #112).
- **The first thing the contact asks is who you are:** "Kaunsa pharma?" (#139), "Sir, Kasrox Pharmaceuticals se baat kar rahe ho Chandigarh se?" (#154), "Haan ji sir, Kesarox se baat kar rahe hain?" (#528).
- **Real leads who start the need themselves** open with territory plus franchise: "Yeh aap PCD dete hain ji? ... Area mera rahega UP mein" (#531); "Sir, aap dawaiyaan bechte ho? ... Haan ji sir, hum PCD franchise dete hain ... Toh sir, yeh Bhopal mein mere district mein mujhe khaali pada hai aapka area?" (#528); "Kuchh chal raha hai idhar Rohtas district mein ... Achha, to dijiye hamako. Kaise kya karna padega? Batayiye." (#295).
- **Existing customers open with the problem, not a greeting:** "Haan bhaiya, kab tak aa jayega maal?" type calls: "Aap phone utha nahi rahe the" (#35), "Aapne mujhe thodi bataya tha ki aapko chahiye ye product" (#121), "Aap phone bhi nahi uthate ... Aap inform toh kar sakte ho" (#505).

### 2.2 Brand name is mis-heard and mis-spelt
Across the transcripts the brand appears as Kesrox 575 times, Kesorx 78, Casrox 37, Kesorox 9, Kasrox 5, plus Kesarox, Castorox, Casrock, Castox ("Catch Rox"). Counted with grep over the master doc. **Add all these spellings as keywords**, and in a WhatsApp chat expect "Kesarox", "Caserox", "Kesrox pharma".

### 2.3 Product names
Contacts name products by a shortened or garbled brand plus a salt/strength. Examples: "625" (Amoxiclav 625; #45, #217, #295), "SP" (Aceclofenac-Paracetamol-Serratiopeptidase; #94, #295 "625 chahiye, SP"), "DSR" (#94), "9G", "Q-zyme/Quozyme", "Spon Cure/Sponcure", "Immigreen/Migraine/Immigrain" (the same product spelt four ways, #246, #283, #289, #414), "K27/Calcium K2-7", "Joint HD / Joint SD", "Roxifit/Roxifort/Roxfit Forte 300ml", "D3 60K", "Pan D / PAN-D", "Cefixime 200", "Deflazacort", "protein powder", "Lycopene softgel", "Electro-Lucid", "Lactulose/Lectulose syrup". They also say the therapy area rather than the product: "enzyme syrup" (#166), "liver syrup" (#386), "Duphalac ka alternative" (#127), "gas tablet", "multivitamin", "drop syrup" (#123), "pet saaf karne ke liye" (#127), "bachchon ki gas syrup" (#510).
**Implication:** the agent should fuzzy-match product names and, when unsure, ask "salt ya strength bataiye (jaise 625, SP, DSR)" and pass the raw text to a human. It must not guess a product.

### 2.4 Territory
- Named as district plus state: Gorakhpur (#78, #167, #206, #482, #504, #527, #541), Rohtas/Buxar/Kaimur (#293, #295), Bhopal (#493, #528), Mohali/Zirakpur (#126), Jodhpur (#170), Muzaffarpur (#178), Akola (#152), Barpeta (#139), Darbhanga (#146, #148), Sitapur (#205), Ayodhya (#267), Kota (#437), Prayagraj (#172), Gwalior (#131).
- The contact wants exclusivity: "Wholesale ya retailer karte ho monopoly mein?" (#172), "monopoly rights" (#205, #504), and cares that nobody else gets the area.
- Savi's real rule from #293, the only genuine monopoly negotiation in the data: she asks **which areas, before they start working there**, writes them in a record, and then guarantees them. "Jo jo aapko area batana padega ... main is jagah pe kaam karunga, phir hum woh likh ke rakhte hain record mein"; "Rohtas jila hai ... pura Rohtas aapka hi rahega"; "Kaimur bhi khali hai ... Bhabhua"; "Agar aapne shuru kar diya hamare sath, toh aapke sath hi rahega." Condition: they must name every district up front.
- Territory may already be taken elsewhere: "Customer ne bataya ki unke area mein pehle se hi monopoly chal rahi hai" (#139 summary), and the contact may already work with 1 to 3 other companies (#154 "teen alag companies", #156, #172 "Prayagraj ... existing vendor happy").

### 2.5 Quantity
Units are cases/boxes/petti, not tablets: "10 petti each for Immigrain and Sponcare, 2 petti for Casro Liv" (#414), "10 cases" (#141, #218), "21 cases" (#262), "17 cases" (#254, #257, #260), "10 boxes ... 625" (#45), "50 dabbe" (#298), "60 box" (#286), "2-3 peti" (#443). Third-party batches are in boxes: minimum "300 box" (#59, #64, #79), "200 boxes" (#58), "500 box" print run (#61, #84), "1000 minimum, food wale" (#189), "150-1000 quantity" (#94, #96).
Small orders exist: Rs 5,000, Rs 8,000 (#451), "5000 se kam bill" (#519), and the contact tries to reach a round case: "bill mein 12 piece kam main add kar deta hoon aur case pura aa jayega" (#246).

### 2.6 Price and rate
- Rates are said as a bare number: "150 ke rate" (#28), "75 ... 70" (#428), "100 rupaye per strip" (#84), "16 rupaye" (#25), "Rs 160 per box" (#20), "240 rupaye box" (#138). These are one-off snapshots from October 2026; do not use them as a price list.
- **Pushback phrasing (the most useful part):**
  - Rate went up: "Existing order ke price increase par concern" (#133); "Q10 ... Rs 200 se 240 ho gaya" (#450); "Client Rs 115 wale multivitamin aur zinc ke rates ko lekar pareshan ... purane rates par maang" (#375).
  - Market quote used as leverage: "market mein log bolte hain, Rs 140 mein mil raha hai, Rs 120 mein mil raha hai" (#295); "Usko Rs 100 kijiye na" (#295).
  - Savi's answer is firm and gives the reason: "Rate increase ho chuka hai, already. Kahin raw material nahi mil raha" (#295); "Nahi ho payega sir"; "rate abhi na badhe hue hain" (#25); she refused 75 to 70 (#428). Where she does move, it is for volume or a repeat customer and always on the bill, e.g. "rates kam karke bill banane ka offer" (#114).
  - Threat to leave: "Mujhe brand withdraw karna padega ... Mera budget itna nahi hai" (#78).
  - Rates too high so purchase paused: "high market rates ki vajah se filhal purchase roke hue hain" (#402).
- Rate comparison between pack sizes is common: "10x10 vs 1x10x10" (#20), "carton vs without-carton" (#96), "mono pack ... thoda rate zyada" (#54), "100ml vs 200ml" (#412).

### 2.7 Credit, advance and payment terms
- **Savi's stated rule is advance:** "Payment advance hai sir" (#295); "ye advance payment ke bina main koi kaam nahi karta ... mera budget exhaust ho jaata hai, mere ko aage payment deni padti hai raw material ke liye manufacturers ko" and "Agar aapko cash pe kaam karna hai toh most welcome" (#451); 50% advance and 50% on delivery was offered to an existing customer and full credit refused (#451 summary); "no advance payment, no work" (#452). Dispatch is held when old dues are unpaid: "pichla payment clear hone par hi naya material dispatch" (#347, #19).
- **Customers ask for credit:** "10-15 din ke extra credit ya limit badhane" (#194, "Yahan toh sab aa ke credit pe hi bikta hai"); "request credit terms for new stock, promising partial payment for previous orders" (#246); pay by instalments with each order, "har naye order ke saath Rs 1000-2000 extra" (#456, #478).
- **The current Q&A says "30% advance, balance on LR copy". I found no call with those numbers.** The calls say "advance", "50/50" (one case) or "cash". Treat 30/70 as unconfirmed.
- Typical excuses when payment is late (they are sincere, so tone matters): bank strike (#436, #440, #441), rain (#388, #442), Puja/Dussehra/Diwali (#153, #158, #192), harvest (#342 "kheti mein busy"), village fair (#196 "gaon mein mela"), accident (#217, #309), death in family (#182, #352), hospital expenses (#451).
- How they pay: "Google Pay, PhonePe, bank transfer ... company ke account mein" (#295); "scanner/QR" and new account numbers (#144, #266, #346, #385, #400), then "screenshot" (#124, #216, #377). Several customers paid into a **retired old account** by mistake (#399, #400, #506), so the agent must never state account details and should point to the invoice/official QR only.

### 2.8 Signals of intent
- **Strong:** names exact products and quantity ("10 petti each", #414); asks for bill/PI ("performa invoice", #20; "PI mangi", #53); gives a dispatch instruction ("aaj hi dispatch karwane ke liye", #329); asks for QR ("payment ke liye QR code manga", #286); asks for transport detail.
- **Medium:** "Rate list WhatsApp par bhej do" (#206), "complete Excel product catalog with prices aur images" (#528), "literature ... kitaab ... kaun kaun sa medicine" (#293).
- **Soft/future:** "filhal nahi hai, future ke liye list bhej do" (#103, #108, #112, #129, #137, #165, #439). About a third of cold leads say this.
- **Verification wanted before buying:** WHO/ISO/GMP certificates (#76, #68, #69), "video call se office dekhna" (#295 summary), "GST ... Chandigarh" and plant name vs company name (#69, #494).

### 2.9 Hesitation and no
"Abhi koi requirement nahi" (#148), "already stock available" (#137), "season off chal raha hai" (#109, #131), "Durga Puja ki wajah se market band" (#158), "teen alag companies ke sath" (#154), "ethical work se mana" (#149), "is segment mein kaam nahi karte" (#142), "wrong number" (#82, #10), "drug license expire ho gaya ... 2-3 saal baad" (#186). Of the 60 sourced leads, 23 declined a current need and 25 accepted a WhatsApp list (grep of summaries; counts overlap).

### 2.10 Urgency
"Aaj hi dispatch" (#16, #329), "5 minute mein godown se dispatch" (#287), "urgent bill banane ke liye" (#71), "D3 60K ki urgent requirement" (#520), "Kesodent syrup ki urgent delivery ... Trackon courier" (#281), "Cefixime ofloxacin jaldi chahiye" (#519). Urgency almost always comes from **existing** customers.

### 2.11 Language and region
- Mostly Hinglish with Devanagari mixed in by the transcriber (#526). English fragments are common in stock phrases: "good morning sir", "any requirement", "no need sir, already stock available sir" (#137).
- Honorifics: "sir/sir ji/bhaiya/madam/Dr. sahab/Tiwari ji". Savi addresses everyone as "sir/ji"; she is called "madam" by some.
- Regional flavour: Bihar/Jharkhand/UP Hindi ("bechint raho", "hamako", "dijiye hamako", "aapka maal chala raha tha"); Bengal and Assam contacts speak simple Hindi with local place names (Hooghly, Burdwan, Raiganj, Nagaon, Hojai, Lakhimpur); South India (Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Andhra) contacts answer in English (#137 "I call you sir, no need sir"; #119, #132). Marathi/Gujarati names appear but calls stayed in Hindi/English. **Rule: reply in the language and script the customer used (Roman Hindi, English, or Devanagari).**
- Timing words: "kal", "parso", "Monday", "shaam ko", "lunch ke baad", "aadhe ghante baad" appear in 157 of 541 transcripts by a loose grep. People think in days of the week and in market hours, not dates. Sunday and Monday transport closures matter (#293).

## 4. Which calls an agent can handle alone, which need a human

### 4.1 Safe for the agent alone (no commitment on money, stock, dates)
1. **Greeting and "who are we"**: Kesrox, Chandigarh (Manimajra), PCD and third-party manufacturing. Basis: #118 "Sir hamara na manufacturing plant hai. Hum monopoly bhi dete provide karte hain PCD pharma franchise aur third party manufacturing bhi karte hain."
2. **Send catalog/product list/brochure** (once the business supplies the files). This is the most common outcome of a cold or soft lead (25 of 60 sourced leads).
3. **Explain the PCD model in plain words** and what marketing material is sent (literature, visual aid, booklet, reminder cards, bag; #293).
4. **Territory enquiry:** collect district, take a note, say the team will confirm availability. Do not say "khaali hai" without a live check.
5. **Who we sell to:** licensed chemist/wholesaler only, no personal-use sales (#41).
6. **Certificates:** acknowledge and promise the documents (WHO-GMP, ISO, GST), then send from an approved file set.
7. **Process questions:** delivery by transport vs courier, typical days, Sunday/Monday closures, bilty arrives on WhatsApp. Only as "usually", with the caveat of stock.
8. **Existing-customer self-service that needs no judgement:** "send me my bilty" is safe only if the agent can look it up; otherwise capture and hand over.
9. **Capture a callback time** and requirement notes.
10. **Polite handling of "abhi nahi, baad mein":** thank, send list, and set a follow-up date. Do not pester in festival weeks.

### 4.2 Needs a human (agent captures and hands over)
| Situation | Why | Calls |
|---|---|---|
| Any rate, discount, "rate kam karo", PI or quotation | Rates are volatile and negotiated, often by MD | #25, #83, #114, #295, #428 |
| Stock availability or dispatch date | Stock changes daily; promises already caused complaints | #34, #35, #345, #505 |
| Credit, instalments, advance exceptions, old dues | MD decides; Savi escalates (#194, #451, #456) | |
| Third-party manufacturing quotation: composition, MOQ, design, cylinder, timeline | Depends on the plant; each is custom | #13, #31, #75, #425 |
| Complaints: damaged, missing, wrong bill, short supply, transport overcharge | Needs photos and a decision | #198, #243, #327, #469, #485 |
| Returns of near-expiry or expired stock | Case by case | #65, #212, #301 |
| Territory confirmation and written monopoly | Binding commitment | #293 |
| Payment confirmation, account change | Fraud/wrong account risk | #399, #400, #506 |
| Angry or grieving contact | Tone and judgement | #115, #116, #182, #217 |
| Any "kya yeh sach me WHO-GMP/same company?" suspicion about the plant | Trust risk | #68, #69 |

### 4.3 Never let the agent do
- State bank/QR/account details (old-account errors, #399, #400).
- Promise "kal dispatch", "isi hafte aayega", "10 baje call aayegi".
- Agree to credit or confirm monopoly.
- Repeat Savi's candid remarks about management or supply ("MD sir ki taraf se supply chain mein delay", #35).

### 4.4 What to capture so the team can follow up next morning
Always capture these fields (ask one at a time, in the contact's language):
1. Name and shop/firm name; is it chemist, wholesaler, distributor, doctor, or a start-up company?
2. Existing Kesrox customer or new? (Match to the CRM contact by number.)
3. District and state; does the contact already have a PCD for the same district elsewhere?
4. Need type: PCD franchise, third-party manufacturing, repeat order, or existing-order issue (payment/dispatch/complaint/return).
5. Products or segments in the contact's own words (antibiotics, pediatric, derma, nutraceutical, "625", "SP", etc.), and strength/pack if known.
6. Quantity in cases/boxes and the batch size for third-party.
7. Drug licence and GST status (yes/no/applying; #85 "drug license apply kar rahe hain").
8. Budget or target rate if they say one.
9. Urgency and best time to call (Savi's contacts ask for evening: "raat ko 9 baje ke baad" #102, "7 se 9 baje ke baad" #155).
10. For existing customers: bill number, order date, product, amount, transport name, photo of damage or screenshot of payment.
11. Preferred language.
12. The exact last message, stored verbatim.

## 5. Where the data does not match the WhatsApp/off-hours reality, and how far to trust the findings

|---|---|---|
| Existing customers (payment, dispatch, stock, bilty, complaint) | 175 | name contains Existing/[E] or tag/summary says existing |
| Internal calls with "Tarun Sir" | 68 | contact name |
| Courier/transport/pickup/Delhivery | 51 | name contains courier/transport/pickup |

Other facts: median call length 44 s; 176 calls are under 30 s. Tag counts of "PCD Monopoly" in the file: 24; third-party manufacturing tag: 38. Call hours: 12 pm to 4 pm are the busiest slots; only 8 calls start at or after 18:00 and none after 20:00.

### 5.2 Mismatches
1. **Direction and initiative.** 539 of 541 are outbound. The two "inbound" ones are a missed call (#125) and #126, which is odd (see 5.3). The WhatsApp agent faces **inbound** messages from people who clicked an ad or saved the number. We have no data on what they type first.
2. **Voice vs text.** Voice allows interruption and tone; WhatsApp contacts send short, broken, one-line or voice-note messages, forward photos, and may write in Roman Hindi with typos. The calls cannot tell us message length, typo patterns, voice-note use or emoji.
3. **Time of day.** There are no off-hours calls. Evidence about night behaviour is limited to two callback requests ("raat ko 9 baje ke baad" #102, "7 se 9 baje ke baad" #155) and Savi herself saying she was busy or travelling. Whether people message at 11 pm for stock or for franchise is unknown.
4. **Lead stage.** The interesting franchise buyers are few. Most data is about people already buying, whose questions (bilty, QR, payment, stock) are different from a first-time lead. An agent trained on this will sound like an order-desk clerk unless the new-lead path is written separately.
5. **Savi's company also buys.** Many "Tarun Sir" calls show Kesrox as a buyer from manufacturers (Ultra, Shivani, Vita, Synergy; #2, #43, #48, #61, #83). Do not mine them for customer behaviour.
6. **The speaker label is unreliable.** In #78 and #451 the "Employee" is actually the customer or the other side. In #101 the AI summary says the "customer called from Casrox Pharmaceuticals", but Savi was the caller. Always read a few lines before quoting.
7. **Rate and stock facts expire.** All prices, availabilities ("Cefixime November ke first week", "Deflazacort 15 November ke baad") are from 5 to 28 October 2026 and are already stale.
8. **Only one person.** Everything reflects Savi's style, pushback and language; Kesrox's other employees may answer differently.

### 7.5 Handoff rules
Handoff means: stop answering, send "Team ko bata diya hai", write a structured note to the CRM (Section 4.4), tag urgency, and notify the right person.

| Trigger | Tag | Owner (to confirm) | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| Existing customer complaint, payment dispute, damage, wrong bill | Complaint | Savi / accounts | High |
| Existing customer: bilty, dispatch, payment confirmation | Order desk | Savi / dispatch | Medium |
| New PCD lead with district + product named | Hot lead | Territory Manager | High (call first) |
| New lead without district, or "list only" | Warm | Telecaller | Normal |
| Third-party enquiry with product/qty | 3P quote | Third-party desk | High |
| Request for rate, credit, discount, MOQ | Pricing | MD / Savi | Normal |
| Hostile, grieving, legal threat | Escalate | MD | High |
| Personal use / non-licensed | Disqualify | None | Low |
| Spam, wrong number, ads | Ignore | None | Low |

Rules: (1) never answer a second time on rate, credit, stock or date; (2) 2 failed attempts to understand = handover; (3) after the second "mujhe sir se baat karni hai" = handover at once; (4) 3 unanswered follow-ups = stop; (5) if the contact is an existing customer with open dues, do not push new offers.
