# What Kesrox's real callers say and ask: learnings for the off-hours WhatsApp agent

## 1. The short version

1. **Savi's 541 calls are mostly not new-lead conversations.** Counting by contact name and tags (rules in section 2), about 167 are existing customers, about 156 are internal, transport, courier, spam or wrong-number calls, and about 218 are everything else. Of those 218, only about 57 carry a lead-source label such as Google, FB or IndiaMART.
2. **The real new-lead exchange is short and one-directional.** Savi calls a form lead with "Koi requirement hai PCD ya third party ke liye?". The lead says "abhi nahi" or "list bhej do". Savi sends a WhatsApp list and asks them to save the number. 31 of the 57 lead-source calls last under 30 seconds.
3. **Most of what customers ask is order logistics.** The big topics are where the goods are (bilty, dispatch), when payment is due, and whether an item is in stock. The agent's first job in off hours is to catch and route these, not to sell.
4. **Of the 5 existing Q&As, none is fully backed by the calls.** Two are partly backed. Three contain claims I could not find anywhere (details in section 7.1).
5. **Several of the most polished PCD "hot leads" look scripted or like tests.** Treat them as weak evidence (section 6).
6. **Do not let the agent quote prices, stock, bank details, credit, or dispatch dates.** In the calls these change week to week, were negotiated by a person, or needed the MD's approval.

## 2. What I read and how I counted

- **Reading.** I read the summaries of calls #1 to #132 in full. For #133 to #541 I read a filtered view that drops calls under 20 seconds, the "Google Drive Sync" boilerplate summaries, "Tarun Sir" internal calls, and personal or wrong-number calls. That view still covers 276 calls. I searched all 541 transcripts by keyword and read about 30 transcripts in part.
- **Call-type split (section 1, point 1).** "Internal/transport/spam" means the contact name contains Tarun Sir, Delhivery, Transport, Courier, TPT, Cargo, Local Pickup, Bigship or Spam, or the tags say Personal, Internal or Wrong Number. "Existing" means "exist" appears in the name or tags. The rest is "other". These are rule-based counts and can be off by a few dozen.
- **Topic counts (section 4).** Each is the number of calls whose tags or summary match a keyword set (for example, payment: "payment"). A call can count in several topics. They show how often a topic comes up and are not mutually exclusive.
- **Speaker labels are unreliable.** In many transcripts "Employee" and "Customer" are swapped, and sometimes Savi is buying from a manufacturer. I use quotes for wording and the summary for who did what. Where the speaker matters, I say so.
- **Dates.** 532 of 541 calls fall in Sep to Oct 2026. The 9 older ones are personal or test recordings (#533 to #541).
- **Hours.** 1 call before 10:00, 8 at or after 18:00, and 5 on a Sunday. No call is a real after-hours conversation.
- **Short transcripts.** 73 transcripts are under 200 characters, so any keyword count undercounts.
- **Phone numbers.** In the transcript file, 539 of 541 contacts show the same placeholder number, so numbers carry no information. I use call numbers and first names only.

### 3.2 How clients name things

- **Products.** They use trade names, shortened and misspelled. Examples: "9G", "Immigreen" / "Imigrain", "Spon Cure" / "Sponecure" / "Spawncure", "K27", "625" (an antibiotic tablet, so "625 ka SP"), "SP", "DSR", "Roxfit", "Kesroclav", "Deflazacort", "Lactulose", "Lycopene softgel".
  - The same product has several spellings in the summaries. The agent must match fuzzily and ask which product is meant if it is unsure.
  - The company is also misspelled. The transcriber wrote Casrox, Kesorx, Castorox, Cassrocs and others. In #152 the lead even spells it out: "Casrox K-E-S-R-O-X". Add these variants to the agent's keywords.
- **Quantities.** "10 petti", "2 case", "50 box", "60 box", "17 cases", "210 units", "300 box minimum" (third-party). The units are petti, case, dabba, box, strip, patti, and nobody uses them consistently.
- **Territory.** A district name plus state: "Gorakhpur", "Rohtas, Buxar, Kaimur", "Akola", "Bhopal", "Jodhpur", "Mohali/Zirakpur". Asked as "Area to bata do sir kaun se state rahega aapka" (#152) and "Area kya rahega, working area jahan pe kaam karte ho aap?" (#178).
- **Price.** Rates are quoted per box, strip or piece and frequently tied to packing. "Mono pack" and "10x10 vs 1x10x10" (#20), "100ml vs 200ml" (#412), "carton vs without carton" (#96). A bare "rate kya hai" is therefore incomplete: the agent needs product and pack.
- **Payment.** "NEFT", "UPI", "QR code", "screenshot", "naye account mein", "cash pe". Customers say "bilty" or "bilti" for the transport receipt, and sometimes "BLT" (#365, #405).

### 3.4 Language and regional variants

- **Language.** Almost all calls are Hinglish in Roman script. Only 9 transcripts are mostly Devanagari. Test calls #526 to #528 and #531 are the ones with Devanagari or English-leaning speech.
- **Regional flavour.** Bihar and Jharkhand speech drops into Bhojpuri/Maithili-flavoured Hindi ("kare ja rahe hain", "hamko", "dijiye hamako" in #295). Assam, West Bengal and Kerala leads are brief, and some answer in English ("Good afternoon, sir"). The agent should reply in the language the lead used and keep sentences short.
- **Place names recur as tags.** Nawada (Bihar) 11 calls, Chandigarh 10, then Gorakhpur, Dhanbad, Ambala. Several are existing customers whose pattern is order, bilty, payment.
- **Honorifics.** "Sir", "Madam" (customers use "sir" for any gender), "ji", and the caller's first name plus "ji".

## 5. What a WhatsApp agent can handle alone, what needs a human, and what to capture

### 5.1 Safe for the agent alone (a fixed answer exists and cannot hurt the business)

1. Greeting, "who are you", "what do you do" (PCD franchise and third-party manufacturing, Chandigarh).
2. Acknowledging a lead and asking the qualification questions in 7.3.
3. "Send me the catalogue / product list": confirm it will be sent, collect the district, and (only if the business has a fixed catalogue file) share it.
4. Whether supply goes to individuals: no, only licensed chemists and wholesalers (#41).
5. What marketing material exists (visual aid, booklet, reminder cards, bag; #293). Do not say "free" until confirmed.
6. How area works: tell us your district, it is recorded, and nobody else is supplied there (#293). Do not promise an area is vacant (see human list).
7. Transport basics: goods go by transport to your nearest transport market or by courier or home delivery; freight is by weight; the order takes about 4 to 5 days excluding Sunday (#293), to be confirmed.
8. Third-party basics: MOQ depends on the product, cylinder and inventory charges apply, lead time is 20 to 25 days (all to be confirmed).
9. "When will someone call?" Give the next-working-day window from the config and do not promise a specific hour.
10. Off-hours acknowledgement and capture for any message that needs a human.

### 5.2 Needs a human (the agent captures and hands off, never answers)

| Trigger | Why (evidence) |
|---|---|
| Any exact rate, discount, scheme, or a request to "reduce the rate" | Rates rose between calls (#133) and are negotiated by hand (#53, #295). |
| Stock, ETA, dispatch date | Stock changes day to day and the answers were guesses ("Monday", "next week"). A wrong date caused the "next week, next week" complaints (#345). |
| Bilty / LR / tracking | Needs the dispatch record. |
| Payment confirmation, bank/QR details, account change | Customers paid into the old account (#400) or hesitated over a QR they did not trust (#147). Never give bank details in chat. |
| Credit, instalments, "pay after delivery" | MD decision (#194, #456). |
| Whether a district is vacant / monopoly confirmed | In the calls Savi usually says "check karke batata hoon" (#528, #531). Availability needs a lookup. |
| Third-party quotes: batch size, formulation, approval, carton design, timeline | Needs a technical and commercial reply (#13, #30, #54, #79). |
| Certificates and "different company name on the WHO certificate" | Caused doubt in #68 and #69; needs a sister-concern explanation by a person. |
| Complaints: delay, damage, overcharge, missing promo items, wrong bill | Emotional and needs a decision (#345, #485, #327, #198). |
| Returns and expiry | Case by case (#65, #121, #212). |
| Anyone who says "MD sir se baat karni hai" or "pehle baat hui thi" | Existing relationship. |
| Anything legal, refund, abusive language, or an owner/senior name | Escalate. |

### 5.3 What to capture so a human can follow up the next morning

Capture these fields in order, stopping once the lead stops answering:
1. Name and business name.
2. Role: chemist, wholesaler, stockist, new entrant, or existing customer (first name or business as shown in the CRM).
3. Need: PCD franchise, third-party manufacturing, product enquiry, order status, payment, complaint, or other.
4. District and state (the area question is asked in 5 of the calls where it was raised, and it drives monopoly).
5. Product or range, with pack size if given.
6. Quantity or order size, if given.
7. Licence status: DL and GST yes or no (the first filter in #41 and #186).
8. Best time for a call and the number or WhatsApp to use. Some leads want evenings, for example "raat ko 9:00 baje ke baad" (#102).
9. Urgency flag (the words "urgent", "aaj", "kal tak").
10. Raw message text, kept word for word.

## 6. Where this data does not match the WhatsApp or off-hours situation, and how much to trust it

| Gap | Effect on trust |
|---|---|
| **No off-hours data.** All calls fall between about 09:00 and 20:00 IST. Only 8 start at or after 18:00. | Nothing here shows what a lead says at 10 pm. What people ask outside hours is an assumption. |
| **No WhatsApp text.** These are phone calls, mostly outbound. 539 are Savi calling, 2 inbound, 1 missed. | Real inbound leads are not represented. Wording in text is shorter and more mixed with English. |
| **Outbound calling to form leads skews the sample.** Most "I have no requirement" replies came from people who had not just asked. | A person who messages you at night is more intent-driven than the lead who answers a cold call. The "no requirement" share (about 40 calls tagged future/season/no requirement) will be lower in a real inbound feed. |
| **Existing customers dominate.** About 167 of 541. They ask about orders, not franchise. | Over-weights logistics and payment. If the WhatsApp number is only for new leads, those questions will be rarer. If it is also the number customers know, they will be common. |
| **Some "hot leads" look scripted or like tests.** #206, #241, #482, #493, #504, #527, #541 (all Gorakhpur or Bhopal, near-identical wording: Cefixime 200mg, Amoxiclav dry syrup, "monopoly confirm"). #526 to #531 are labelled "Kanu" or "Kartik (Samsung Drive Test)"; #531 opens with "Alota speaking". #126 is also very polished. | Do not use them to claim real demand, or to claim Savi's answers. In them the 25,000 to 30,000 minimum is stated only in #527. |
| **Transcripts are garbled and speaker labels swap.** | Treat figures from transcripts as indicative. Verify any number you will put in an answer. |
| **Summaries are AI-written.** They sometimes misread the company name or the topic (for example "Casrox" for Kesrox, and "Bill of Lading" for bilty in #365). | Quote the transcript, not the summary, whenever a quote matters. |
| **Seasonality.** Many "no requirement" answers cite season, Puja, Diwali, bank strike, rain. | These shift the mix across months. Only Sep to Oct 2026 is covered. |
| **The CRM's 2,333 WhatsApp "ad leads"** | Machine-generated, not used here. |

Net: **trust the data most for (a) how customers phrase product, quantity, area and payment, and (b) what operational questions repeat. Trust it least for lead volume, conversion rates, and any rate, MOQ or policy figure.**

### 7.5 Handoff rules (put in the system prompt)

1. If the message is about a rate, discount, scheme, stock, ETA, bilty, payment confirmation, credit, a complaint, a return, or a certificate: reply with a short acknowledgement, collect the 5.3 fields, set `handoff = true`, and do not answer the substance.
2. If the lead asks about monopoly or area availability: record the district, give the standard line, hand off.
3. If the sender is an existing customer (name matches the CRM or says "pehle order diya tha"): skip qualification, capture the order, bill or dispatch reference, hand off.
4. If the message contains "urgent", "aaj", "kal tak", or anger: tag high priority and put it at the top of the morning list.
5. If asked something not in the Q&A: say the team will confirm, capture it, hand off. Do not guess.
6. If the sender goes silent after the first message: no repeated nudges; one next-morning follow-up by a human.
7. Never send bank details or QR codes. Never confirm a payment.

### 7.6 The fixed night reply

The current fixed reminder promises a callback at 10:00 AM. The calls show callbacks slipping ("next week" in #345, "chutti par the" in #350, holidays and bank strikes in October). I suggest replacing the hour with a window and keeping the message short:
